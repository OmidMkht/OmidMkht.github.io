Omid Mokhtari

Ph.D. student in Electrical Engineering · University of Vermont

Burlington, Vermont

I am a Ph.D. student in Electrical Engineering at the University of Vermont, advised by Mads R. Almassalkhi and Samuel Chevalier. I expect to complete my Ph.D. in Fall 2027 and am looking for postdoctoral positions.

My research makes large power-grid models small enough to compute with, without losing the accuracy engineers need. Real distribution feeders can have thousands of nodes, which makes many optimization and control problems slow or impractical to solve. I develop optimization methods that shrink these network models while keeping a guaranteed bound on voltage error. For example, on real utility feeders with 3,499 to 8,381 nodes, my methods remove 80–94% of the nodes while keeping the voltage error below 0.3% of nominal voltage.

Research

Optimal network reduction. Opti-KRON uses optimization to decide which nodes of a power network to merge, so the smaller model still reproduces the original voltages. I have developed versions for radial distribution networks and for unbalanced three-phase feeders.

Scalable computation. To handle networks with thousands of nodes, I split them into communities that are reduced in parallel, and I run the reduction on GPUs (up to 15× faster than on CPUs in our tests).

Estimation with few sensors. With collaborators, I study data-driven power flow and sensor placement, which estimate grid voltages with errors below 0.1% when only about a quarter of locations have sensors.

A little more about me

Before coming to Vermont, I earned an M.Sc. in Electrical Engineering (power systems) at Sharif University of Technology, where I worked on co-planning merchant energy storage with transmission systems. I received my B.Sc. in Electrical Engineering from Imam Khomeini International University in Qazvin, Iran.
